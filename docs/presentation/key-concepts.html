mote

Characters that live with you and get things done.

Twenty promises. What they do for you, not how they are built. mote is the engine they run on.

Open source. Runs on your hardware and your subscriptions. Planning finished 2026-10-08.

01

It answers instantly.

A word back in about 300 milliseconds, from your own hardware, even offline.

02

It knows when you are talking to it.

Sure, it answers. Half sure, it asks. Otherwise it stays out of the way.

03

It texts like a person.

Sent, delivered, read, typing. The chat you already know.

04

It has a character you choose.

Voice, tone and name are options, not a fixed persona.

05

It thinks only when it has to.

Hard questions get a real thinking model. Easy ones cost nothing.

06

It gets things done, not just answered.

Code, research, errands across your tools, carried end to end.

07

It keeps going.

Blocked, it looks for another way and lines up alternatives. It waits for you only when it must.

08

It works with your computer off.

The device stands on its own. When your computer is on, it connects for more power, with options you control.

09

It asks before acting outward.

Nothing is posted, sent or deleted without your approval.

10

It knows what it does not know.

When it cannot see something or lacks access, it says so and names what it needs.

11

It can reach everything you can.

Slack, mail, calendar, files, code, the web. All of it, efficiently, through one context layer.

12

It remembers properly.

Facts with dates, people, projects, and how they changed. Yours, not hosted.

13

It can tell you what it knew.

Every answer can show what it saw and what it cost.

14

It draws its own screens.

A reply is an interface, not a chat bubble. Lists, forms, progress, on a touchscreen or a window.

15

It lives on a small device.

A Pi with a touchscreen on the desk, a window on the Mac, your phone. One agent.

16

It never loses a thread.

Close it, switch devices, lose Wi-Fi. It picks up where it was.

17

It runs on what you already pay for.

Your subscriptions, your hardware. No API keys, no per-token bill. Open source.

18

It can use a browser.

In the cloud when your computer is off. Through your own signed-in sessions when it is on. It fills forms and buys nothing without asking.

19

It can talk.

Voice in and out, hands free. Later.

20

You can open the hood while it runs.

Click any stage to see what it is doing and change it, down to the model behind it. No restart.

How it is built

One daemon in Rust, on a machine you pick.

Promises 1, 5, 17 are the talk lane, the router and the think lane: a local model first, a thinking model on your subscription when needed. Promises 6, 7, 8, 18 are the work lane, the persistence loop and the browser: the device alone, or your Mac through Overseer when it is on. Promises 10, 11 are the context layer: connectors, an index, retrieval, consumption and a coverage map of what it can and cannot reach. Promises 12, 13, 16 are the memory graph, the context ledger and the event log. Promises 3, 14, 15 are the chat surface and a small UI language rendered straight to pixels on any screen.
